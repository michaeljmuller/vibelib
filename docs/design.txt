The database should keep extracted metadata separate from data that is synthesized, downloaded, or manually entered.

For example, an ebook might say the author is James SA Corey.  Another ebook may have James S. A. Corey.  I will maintain that
inconsistency on those ebooks' metadata.  A separate table of authors will have a normalized representation of that spelling.

I will maintain an "is pseudonym for" table; this may be a one-to-one relationship (Richard Bachman -> Stephen King) or a
one-to-many relationship (James SA Corey --> Daniel Abraham, James SA Corey --> Ty Franck).  This allows me to indicate that
James SA Corey is a pseudonym for two people.

NOTE: This modeling FAILS to capture "house novel" situation, when multiple books in a series are written by different authors
under the same pseudonym, as with the Nancy Drew and Hardy Boys books (which are written by several different people under the
same name).  In order to capture that, I'd need some sort of way to capture/represent that author A is a
pesudonuym for B *or* C, as well as a way to capture/represent that author X is a pseudonym for Y *and* Z.  Plus I'd need an
option "actually written by" relationship from book to author to capture the actual author of the book.  I choose to ignore
this complexity for now.

I will also have an abstract representation of a book.  I will recognize that "We are Legion.epub" and "We are Legion (We
are Bob).m4b" are the same book in different formats.

I will have an abstract representation of series of books.  The books will have an OPTIONAL series number.  The series number
will be represented as an integer (whole number, no decimal).  Interstitial books (like Edgedancer in the Stormlight archives)
can have no series number and their position can be inferred based on publication date, if desired.  Or they can be left off
the series, and just considered books in the same "universe".  I am not going to try to represent universes.
